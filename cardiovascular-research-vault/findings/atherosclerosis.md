# Atherosclerosis

## In my words
A chronic inflammatory disease of the arterial wall, not just "pipe furring."
Retained apoB lipoproteins (LDL and friends) accumulate in the intima, are
modified, and trigger an immune response — macrophages become foam cells, a lipid
core forms under a fibrous cap. It's a wall biology problem whose *luminal*
narrowing is a late, partial readout. This is the substrate behind
[[vulnerable-plaque]] and the reason [[ldl-cholesterol-causality]] matters.

## Why it matters
Framing it as inflammation-on-a-lipid-substrate explains why events come from
plaques that weren't the tightest stenosis, and why lowering apoB changes
trajectory. It separates *disease burden* (wall) from *flow limitation* (lumen) —
the same anatomy-vs-physiology split that [[fractional-flow-reserve]] formalizes.

## Strength & caveats
Core biology well supported. Open edges: how much residual risk is inflammatory vs.
lipid, and which imaging marker of wall disease actually predicts events. Don't
equate angiographic stenosis with disease activity.

## Connections
- [[ldl-cholesterol-causality]] — *driven-by*: apoB lipoprotein retention is the
  causal initiator of the wall disease.
- [[vulnerable-plaque]] — *progresses-to*: the dangerous, rupture-prone end of the
  same process.
- [[fractional-flow-reserve]] — *distinct-from*: FFR measures flow limitation (lumen);
  atherosclerosis is wall burden — different axes that can diverge.
- [[ischemia-guided-revascularization]] — *explains*: why event prevention tracks
  wall biology (lipid lowering) more than fixing focal stenoses.

## Questions
- If disease activity lives in the wall but we triage on the lumen, design a study
  that tests whether a wall-imaging marker adds event prediction beyond stenosis
  severity.
- Why can two patients with identical angiographic stenosis have very different event
  risk? Name the variable and how you'd measure it.
