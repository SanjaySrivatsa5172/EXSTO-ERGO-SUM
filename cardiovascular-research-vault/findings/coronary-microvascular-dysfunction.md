# Coronary Microvascular Dysfunction (CMD)

## In my words
Ischemia originating in vessels too small to see on angiography — impaired
vasodilator capacity and/or increased microvascular resistance of the coronary
microcirculation. It's a major reason patients have angina and ischemia with
*non-obstructive* coronary arteries (ANOCA/INOCA). Measured by coronary flow
reserve (CFR) and the index of microcirculatory resistance (IMR), not by epicardial
narrowing.

## Why it matters
CMD breaks the assumption baked into epicardial-lesion thinking: a normal
[[fractional-flow-reserve]] can coexist with real, microvascular ischemia. It
reframes "normal angiogram + symptoms" from "non-cardiac" to a measurable disease,
and may explain part of the residual ischemia/symptoms left after focusing only on
flow-limiting epicardial lesions ([[ischemia-guided-revascularization]]).

## Strength & caveats
Increasingly accepted as a real entity with prognostic weight, but: CFR/IMR cutoffs
are conventions, measurement is technique-sensitive, and endotypes (structural vs.
functional, vasospastic) likely respond differently to therapy. Trial evidence for
*treatment* benefit is still maturing — don't overstate.

## Connections
- [[fractional-flow-reserve]] — *confounds*: CMD violates FFR's epicardial assumption;
  normal FFR can hide microvascular ischemia.
- [[ischemia-guided-revascularization]] — *residual-cause*: explains part of the
  ischemia/symptoms epicardial revascularization leaves behind.
- [[atherosclerosis]] — *parallel-disease*: can coexist with or be independent of
  epicardial plaque burden.
- [[vulnerable-plaque]] — *distinct-axis*: a different mechanism of ischemia/events not
  captured by plaque imaging.
