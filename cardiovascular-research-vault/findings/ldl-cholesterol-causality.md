# LDL / ApoB Causality

## In my words
LDL (more precisely apoB-containing lipoproteins) is not just a marker but a *causal*
driver of [[atherosclerosis]], supported by convergence across lines of evidence:
Mendelian randomization (lifelong genetic low-LDL → low risk), dose-response across
statin/ezetimibe/PCSK9 trials, and a consistent "lower is better, longer is better"
relationship. Cumulative exposure (LDL × time) matters, not just a single level.

## Why it matters
Causality + cumulative exposure is what justifies treating LDL early and to low
targets for *event prevention* — the job that [[ischemia-guided-revascularization]]
showed revascularization mostly doesn't do in stable disease. It's the strongest
case that the lever for hard outcomes is the wall biology, not the lumen.

## Strength & caveats
Among the best-supported causal claims in cardiology. Open edges: residual risk
after maximal LDL lowering (inflammatory, Lp(a), triglyceride-rich remnants), and
where the benefit/cost curve flattens at very low levels. Causal for *atherosclerosis*
is not the same as predictive for an individual [[vulnerable-plaque]] rupturing.

## Connections
- [[atherosclerosis]] — *causes*: apoB retention initiates and sustains the wall disease.
- [[vulnerable-plaque]] — *stabilizes*: lowering apoB shifts plaque composition away from
  rupture-prone.
- [[ischemia-guided-revascularization]] — *complements*: provides the hard-outcome lever
  that revascularization mostly doesn't in stable CAD.
- [[coronary-microvascular-dysfunction]] — *distinct-from*: residual risk/ischemia not
  addressed by LDL lowering alone.

## Questions
- Mendelian randomization supports lifelong low LDL → low risk. Why is that evidence
  stronger for causality than even a positive statin RCT, and where does the MR argument
  have limits?
- "Lower for longer" implies cumulative exposure. Design an analysis that tests whether
  area-under-the-LDL-curve predicts events better than a current single level.
