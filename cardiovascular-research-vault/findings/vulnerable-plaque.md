# Vulnerable Plaque

## In my words
A plaque biologically prone to rupture/erosion and thrombosis rather than one that
is merely large — classically a thin-cap fibroatheroma: big lipid core, thin
inflamed fibrous cap, often only modestly stenotic. The concept explains the
clinical paradox that many infarcts arise from lesions that looked non-obstructive
on prior angiography. It's the dangerous end of [[atherosclerosis]].

## Why it matters
If risk lives in plaque *composition*, not lumen diameter, then an
angiography/physiology-guided strategy aimed at flow-limiting lesions
([[fractional-flow-reserve]], [[ischemia-guided-revascularization]]) can miss the
lesions that actually cause events — a genuine tension in how we triage.

## Strength & caveats
The construct is real but **prediction is weak**: imaging-identified vulnerable
plaques have low positive predictive value for events at the individual-lesion
level (most don't rupture; many ruptures are clinically silent). Treat "vulnerable
plaque" as a population concept, not a reliable per-lesion forecast.

## Connections
- [[atherosclerosis]] — *subtype-of*: the rupture-prone phenotype of the wall disease.
- [[fractional-flow-reserve]] — *contradicts/orthogonal*: dangerous plaques are often
  not flow-limiting, so an FFR-guided strategy can pass over them.
- [[ischemia-guided-revascularization]] — *explains-tension*: events from non-obstructive
  lesions are part of why fixing ischemia doesn't prevent many MIs.
- [[ldl-cholesterol-causality]] — *modified-by*: lipid lowering stabilizes/regresses the
  composition that makes a plaque vulnerable.

## Questions
- Per-lesion PPV for vulnerable plaque is low. What does that imply for any trial
  proposing to treat imaging-identified plaques, and how would you power it?
- If most ruptures are silent, how would you design an endpoint that captures
  plaque-stabilization benefit without being swamped by clinically irrelevant events?
