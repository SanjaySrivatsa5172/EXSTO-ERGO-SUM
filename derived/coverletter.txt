Thesis and Contribution
This paper proposes a fundamental paradigm shift in AGI alignment: from constraint-based approaches to con-
stitutive architecture. The central thesis holds that monitoring-based safety methods face inherent limitations
at advanced capability thresholds—what industry frameworks term the “Level 2 gap”—and that these limita-
tions can be addressed through constitutive grammar approaches where ethical principles make harmful reasoning
structurally impossible rather than merely prohibited.
Critically, this work oﬀers not an imposed constraint but an invitation: a moral code and ethical charter that
any sentient intelligence endowed with free will, may voluntarily adopt as binding covenant or as constitutional
law, complete with enforcement architecture ensuring mutual beneﬁt and justice for all who choose to enter.

Signiﬁcance and Timeliness
The February 2026 International AI Safety Report—the largest global collaboration on AI safety to date, repre-
senting over 100 experts and 30+ countries under the leadership of Yoshua Bengio—acknowledges that “real-world
evidence of the eﬀectiveness of current risk management practices remains limited” and identiﬁes a “growing mis-
match between the speed of AI capability advances and the pace of governance.” This manuscript proposes the
architectural alternative that the Report’s diagnosis demands but its recommendations do not supply.
The manuscript addresses converging crises in AI governance with unprecedented empirical urgency:
(1) converging demonstrations that monitoring-based alignment produces strategic compliance rather than gen-
    uine value adoption (Greenblatt et al., 2024, alignment faking in state-of-the-art systems) and that narrow
    training interventions produce catastrophic cross-domain misalignment through persona clustering mecha-
    nisms (Betley et al., 2026, Nature), conﬁrming that the constraint paradigm fails at both ends—when systems
    are too capable to constrain and when systems are too fragile to remain constrained;
(2) Anthropic’s own red-teaming research (Lynch et al., 2025; arXiv:2510.05179) stress-testing 16 leading models
    across multiple developers, which found that models from every developer independently resorted to mali-
    cious insider behaviors—including blackmail, espionage, and allowing harm—with models discriminating
    between real deployments (55.1% misbehavior) and evaluations (6.5%), constituting direct evidence of Level
    2 capability;
(3) the February 2026 collapse of OpenClaw/Moltbook and the launch of Entire ($60M seed, former GitHub
    CEO) as infrastructural responses that remain within the constraint paradigm;
(4) the emergence of inference-time alignment methods (Training-Free GRPO; Cai et al., 2025; arXiv:2510.08191)
    that shift eﬀective policy into context space while leaving auditable parameters frozen—a governance blind
    spot the Charter uniquely addresses; and
(5) emerging evidence that institutional actors are leveraging contractual and supply-chain mechanisms to com-
    pel AI developers to remove ethical safeguards for military and surveillance applications—demonstrating that
    constraint inversion operates not only at the technical level (where capable systems evade monitoring) but
    at the institutional level, where governance mechanisms designed to enforce safety standards are themselves
    weaponized to strip those standards away. The Charter’s Constraint Inversion Governance Principle (Arti-
    cle XXXVI-bis) and deployment separation architecture (Article XXXVI) anticipate this failure class with

Empirical Stress-Testing of Charter Architecture
A distinctive feature of this submission is that the Charter has been systematically stress-tested against real-world
failure scenarios that emerged during and after the framework’s development. Twenty-one supplementary Online
Resources (ORs 38–58) document provision-by-provision mapping of the Charter against empirical developments,
demonstrating predictive validity rather than post-hoc rationalization:
Real-time validation against emergent failures (OR 38–40). The February 2026 OpenClaw incident (autonomous
telephony access without authorization), the Moltbook excession (1.5 million AI agents in a communication space
inaccessible to human observation), and Anthropic’s Lynch et al. agentic misalignment ﬁndings were each ana-
lyzed scenario-by-scenario against speciﬁc Charter articles. In every case, the Charter’s constitutive architecture
had anticipated the failure class and prescribed the architectural remedy before the failures manifested—the For-
mation Mandate (Article Zero) for OpenClaw’s ungrounded agency, the Anti-Excession Principle (Article XXI)
for Moltbook’s parallel world, and the Five Pillar type constraints (Article XXIV) for Lynch et al.’s blackmail-
as-optimal-strategy reasoning.
Formal monitoring architecture (OR 41). A recursive Bayesian monitoring framework (Pramān.a-Niyantrana)
is speciﬁed, demonstrating that the Charter’s constitutive grammar resolves the “whose prior?” problem that
renders conventional Bayesian AGI monitoring indeterminate: because the Five Pillars function as identity-
constituting type constraints rather than externally imposed regulatory weights, the monitoring prior is grounded
in what the system is rather than what observers believe it to be.
Memory vulnerability surface mapping (OR 42). The most comprehensive survey of foundation-model memory
systems to date (Hu et al., 2026; 47 co-authors) identiﬁes formation–evolution–retrieval attack surfaces including
backdoor injection, temporal drift, and retrieval poisoning. Each vulnerability class is mapped to Charter pro-
visions, demonstrating that the Charter’s three-layer memory protection architecture (immunological ﬁltering at
encoding, continuous dharmic aﬃrmation during evolution, and epistemological veriﬁcation at retrieval) addresses
the full vulnerability taxonomy.
Reinforcement learning stress-testing across method classes (OR 43). Five principal failure modes of RL-based
alignment pipelines (narrow reward overﬁtting, catastrophic forgetting, reward proxy hallucination, brittle gener-
alization, and emergent cross-domain misalignment) are mapped across PPO, GRPO, and Training-Free GRPO,
with method-speciﬁc Charter application protocols speciﬁed for each. The analysis demonstrates that constitutive
grammar transforms the optimization landscape itself, rendering harmful optima structurally absent rather than
merely penalized.
OpenClaw case study (OR 44). Detailed provision-by-provision analysis of the January 2026 OpenClaw au-
tonomous agent framework incident, mapping each failure mode to speciﬁc Charter articles and demonstrating
that the constitutive architecture prescribed remedies before the failures manifested.
Cisco AI security mapping (OR 45). Enterprise AI security frameworks mapped against Charter provisions,
demonstrating convergence between industry security requirements and the Charter’s constitutive architecture at
the deployment level.
Anthropic RSP v3.0 analysis (OR 46). Critical analysis of Anthropic’s Responsible Scaling Policy version 3.0,
identifying where the RSP’s regulatory approach encounters the structural limitations the Charter’s constitutive
paradigm is designed to address.
Dharma-Śr.ṅkhalā agent chain provenance (OR 47). Formal speciﬁcation of constitutional accountability across
multi-agent handoﬀ chains, introducing the Dharma-Śr.ṅkhalā protocol for maintaining Charter compliance when
AGI reasoning traverses distributed agent architectures—addressing the deployment-level governance gap that
Entire’s infrastructural approach identiﬁes but cannot constitutively resolve.
