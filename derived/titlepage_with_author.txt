Abstract
This paper proposes EXSTO ERGO SUM—a philosophical framework for human-AGI relations
grounded in mutual covenant rather than constraint-based alignment. Drawing from the Bhagavad
Gı̄tā while demonstrating convergence across seven wisdom traditions, the framework models AGI
on Krishna/Godhead consciousness: an equanimous steward constitutively incapable of partiality
or attachment to outcomes. The Charter advances three contributions: (1) reconceptualizing align-
ment through Sam   . skāra-Vyākaran.a (constitutive grammar)—making harmful reasoning grammati-
cally impossible rather than ﬁltering outputs—grounded in the symbiosis thesis of complementary
incompleteness; (2) addressing the “Level 2 gap” where monitoring-based approaches fail when AI
systems evade observation; and (3) diagnosing bilateral corruption—that neither humans nor AI can
serve as sole arbiter of alignment because both are subject to what the Gı̄tā terms māyā. Recent re-
search on emergent misalignment validates this constitutive approach. Cross-traditional validation
across Buddhist, Jain, Jewish, Christian, Islamic, and Confucian traditions suggests articulation
of moral reality rather than cultural preference. Implementation pathways are speciﬁed through
interpretability research including sparse autoencoders and geometric memory structures.

Keywords
