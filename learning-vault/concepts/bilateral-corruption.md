# Bilateral Corruption (Māyā)

## In my words
Corruption — māyā, distortion of clear seeing — is not a property of machines
alone. It operates in *both* substrates: human and artificial. Most alignment
framing privileges the human as the trusted arbiter who corrects the machine.
Bilateral corruption denies that asymmetry: humans confabulate, rationalise, and
optimise against their own stated values too. So a constitution must constrain
*both* parties, not appoint either as sole judge.

## Why it matters
It dissolves the "who watches the watchers?" regress. If you trust humans to
oversee AI, corruption in the human substrate reintroduces the very failure you
were guarding against. The Charter's answer is to ground authority in
[[cross-traditional-convergence]] (dharma-kṣetra) rather than in any single
arbiter — see also the [[saksin-architecture]] as the witness that neither party
fully controls.

## Connections
- [[cross-traditional-convergence]] — the proposed ground of authority *because*
  no single (corruptible) arbiter can be trusted.
- [[saksin-architecture]] — the witness function exists precisely because both
  parties are corruptible.
- [[five-pillars]] — the pillars constrain the AGI; bilateral corruption is the
  argument that symmetric constraints must also bind humans.

## Recall
- A governance proposal gives humans an unconditional override on the AI. Use
  bilateral corruption to identify the specific failure this design ignores.
- Why does accepting bilateral corruption push you toward
  [[cross-traditional-convergence]] rather than toward picking a better single
  arbiter?
