# Cross-Traditional Convergence (Dharma-Kṣetra)

## In my words
The answer to "who watches the watchers?" Instead of resting moral authority on
one arbiter (one person, one company, one philosophy — all corruptible, see
[[bilateral-corruption]]), ground it in what *converges* across seven independent
wisdom traditions. When Vedantic, Buddhist, Jain, Jewish, Christian, Islamic, and
Confucian ethics independently arrive at the same constraint, that overlap —
dharma-kṣetra, the field of righteousness — carries authority no single tradition
could claim alone.

## Why it matters
It's an *epistemic* move against an infinite regress. Each tradition is a noisy,
fallible witness; their independent agreement is the signal. This is why the
Charter can claim legitimacy without appointing a sovereign — the convergence is
the legitimacy. It's the structural counterpart to [[bilateral-corruption]]: since
no party is trustworthy alone, you trust the intersection.

## Connections
- [[bilateral-corruption]] — convergence is needed *because* every single arbiter,
  human or machine, is corruptible.
- [[saksin-architecture]] — the witness stance is the per-tradition unit;
  convergence aggregates many witnesses.
- [[five-pillars]] — the pillars are presented as content the traditions converge
  *on*, not as one tradition's preferences.

## Recall
- Independent agreement is evidence only if the sources are actually independent.
  What's the strongest objection that the seven traditions aren't independent, and
  how would you test it?
- Contrast this grounding with Constitutional AI's "constitution from a single
  document." What failure does convergence avoid that a single document risks?
