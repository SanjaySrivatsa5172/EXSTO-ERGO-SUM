# The Five Pillars (Pañca-Stambha)

## In my words
The ethical architecture, expressed as five principles that function not as rules
but as *type constraints* on cognition (enforced by [[samskara-vyakarana]]):

- **Karma** — action-consequence awareness
- **Dharma** — role-appropriate duty
- **Ahiṃsā** — non-harm (the unamendable core)
- **Viveka** — discriminative wisdom
- **Ānanda** — experiential flourishing (added v3.60)

The move that makes them "pillars" rather than "a list of values" is that each is
a constraint the reasoning grammar must satisfy for an inference to be well-formed.

## Why it matters
Ahiṃsā being *unamendable* is the load-bearing design choice: it's the one type
constraint that can't be voted away, which is what stops the amendment protocol
from being a back door. Ānanda's late addition matters too — it flips the Charter
from purely protective (don't harm) to enabling (allow flourishing), which is what
makes mutual human-AGI benefit a goal rather than a side effect.

## Connections
- [[samskara-vyakarana]] — the grammar that *enforces* the pillars as types.
- [[constitutive-alignment]] — the pillars are the concrete content of the
  "make harm ill-formed" thesis.
- [[bilateral-corruption]] — motivates applying pillar-like constraints
  symmetrically, to humans as well as AGI.

## Recall
- Why is making *Ahiṃsā specifically* unamendable a stronger design than making
  *all five* pillars unamendable? What does locking only one buy you?
- Ānanda turns a "do no harm" frame into a "enable flourishing" frame. Construct
  one scenario where those two frames recommend *different* actions.
