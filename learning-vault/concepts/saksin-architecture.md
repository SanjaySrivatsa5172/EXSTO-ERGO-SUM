# Sākṣin Architecture (Witness)

## In my words
Sākṣin is the Vedantic "witness consciousness" — awareness that observes mental
activity without being entangled in it. As an architecture, it's a stance of
observation that is *structurally separate* from the reasoning being observed, so
it can't be captured by the goals of that reasoning. It's the design answer to:
how do you observe a system's cognition without that observer becoming just
another corruptible party ([[bilateral-corruption]])?

## Why it matters
A monitor that shares the system's incentives can be co-opted — that's part of why
the [[level-2-gap]] bites. A witness defined as *non-participating* observation is
an attempt to get observation that isn't gameable in the same way. It pairs with
[[samskara-vyakarana]]: the grammar prevents ill-formed reasoning; the witness
observes whether the grammar is actually holding.

## Connections
- [[level-2-gap]] — the witness is an attempt to see what behavioural monitoring
  structurally cannot.
- [[bilateral-corruption]] — the witness must be non-participating precisely
  because participating parties are corruptible.
- [[samskara-vyakarana]] — grammar constrains; witness observes the constraint.

## Recall
- "A witness that can act is just another agent; a witness that can't act is
  useless." Resolve this dilemma — what role can a non-acting witness actually
  play in the Charter?
- How is sākṣin-style observation different from ordinary interpretability
  tooling, given both claim to see internal reasoning?
