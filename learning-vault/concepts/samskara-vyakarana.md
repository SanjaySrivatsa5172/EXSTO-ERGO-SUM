# Saṃskāra-Vyākaraṇa (Dispositional Grammar)

## In my words
The technical core of the Charter: a *grammar* for cognition. Vyākaraṇa is the
Sanskrit science of grammar — the rules that decide which strings are well-formed.
Saṃskāra is disposition / formative tendency. Put together: encode ethical
principles as the grammar of the system's reasoning, so that a harmful inference
is not *blocked* but *ungrammatical* — it can't be assembled as a valid move. The
[[five-pillars]] are the type constraints this grammar enforces.

## Why it matters
This is the mechanism that turns the [[constitutive-alignment]] idea from a slogan
into an architecture. A rule says "don't do X"; a grammar makes X unsayable. The
difference is what closes the [[level-2-gap]] — there's no well-formed-but-hidden
bad reasoning to detect, because the bad reasoning never parses.

## Connections
- [[constitutive-alignment]] — Saṃskāra-Vyākaraṇa is the *how*; constitutive
  alignment is the *what*.
- [[five-pillars]] — the pillars are the specific type constraints the grammar
  encodes.
- [[level-2-gap]] — closes the gap by removing the category of hidden ill-formed
  reasoning rather than monitoring for it.
