# Ambulatory Venous Pressure (AVP) & the Calf Pump

## In my words
On walking, the calf-muscle pump squeezes deep veins and drives blood upward;
competent valves keep it from falling back, so venous pressure at the ankle
*drops*. AVP is that lowered pressure during exercise; venous refilling time is how
long pressure takes to return to baseline after stopping. In disease, the pump
can't lower pressure (or it refills too fast through [[venous-reflux]]) — this is
the quantitative definition of the ambulatory venous hypertension underlying
[[chronic-venous-insufficiency]].

## Why it matters
It's the closest thing to a *direct* hemodynamic outcome — the actual quantity
treatment is trying to change. Reflux maps and CEAP class are proxies; AVP and
refilling time measure the pressure itself. That makes it valuable for research
endpoints, even if invasiveness limits routine clinical use.

## Strength & caveats
Classic physiology, but AVP is invasive (needle in a dorsal foot vein), so most
clinical work uses non-invasive surrogates (air/photo-plethysmography) that
correlate imperfectly. Reference ranges vary by technique and posture — verify
before using as an endpoint, and don't treat a surrogate as if it were AVP.

## Connections
- [[chronic-venous-insufficiency]] — *defines*: CVI is clinically the consequence of
  sustained ambulatory venous hypertension — what AVP measures.
- [[venous-reflux]] — *measures*: shortened venous refilling time is reflux's
  hemodynamic signature.
- [[endovenous-ablation]] — *outcome-for*: AVP/refilling time is a candidate endpoint
  for whether treatment changed the pressure, not just the anatomy.
- [[ceap-classification]] — *complements*: CEAP describes; AVP quantifies — useful when
  an ordinal C-class is too coarse for an endpoint.
- [[reflux-severity-evaluation]] — *anchors*: AVP is the reference standard for the
  hemodynamic axis of reflux severity.

## Questions
- AVP is invasive, so studies lean on plethysmographic surrogates that correlate
  imperfectly. What does using a surrogate endpoint do to the sample size you need to
  detect a real treatment effect, and why?
- Could a treatment improve symptoms without changing AVP (or vice versa)? Give a
  mechanism for each direction and what it implies about endpoint selection.
