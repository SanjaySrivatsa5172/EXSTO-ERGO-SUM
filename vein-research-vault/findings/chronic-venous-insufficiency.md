# Chronic Venous Insufficiency (CVI)

## In my words
A state where the venous system of the lower limb fails to return blood
efficiently, so venous pressure stays pathologically high during standing and
walking (ambulatory venous hypertension). The downstream of that sustained
pressure is the clinical picture — oedema, skin changes, ulceration. The usual
driver is [[venous-reflux]] (valves failing), but obstruction
([[may-thurner-syndrome]]) or calf-pump failure can produce or worsen it.

## Why it matters
It reframes "varicose veins" from a cosmetic problem to a *hemodynamic* one: the
treatable target is the pressure, and the pressure is driven by reflux and/or
obstruction. That's what justifies intervening on the underlying circuit rather
than only treating surface veins.

## Strength & caveats
Concept-level, well established. The contested parts are quantitative: which
pressure/duration thresholds predict skin damage, and how much of advanced CVI is
reflux vs. obstruction vs. both. Treat any single mechanism as incomplete.

## Connections
- [[venous-reflux]] — *mechanism-for*: reflux is the commonest cause of the
  ambulatory venous hypertension that defines CVI.
- [[may-thurner-syndrome]] — *mechanism-for (alternative)*: obstruction can cause
  the same picture without reflux — the competing arm of the P axis.
- [[ambulatory-venous-pressure]] — *refines*: AVP is the direct measurement of the
  pressure CVI is defined by; CVI is the clinical face, AVP the quantity.
- [[ceap-classification]] — *describes*: CEAP is how a given CVI limb is staged.
