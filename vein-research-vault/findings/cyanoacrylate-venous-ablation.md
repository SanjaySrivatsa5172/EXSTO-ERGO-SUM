# Cyanoacrylate (Adhesive) Venous Ablation

> **Interpretation flag:** created from "accurate venous ablation" in the seed list,
> read as **cyanoacrylate glue closure** (the non-thermal modality that fits with
> thermal/RF/laser). Rename if you meant something else.

## In my words
A **non-thermal, non-tumescent (NTNT)** closure: medical adhesive (cyanoacrylate,
e.g. VenaSeal) is injected to glue the vein walls together, occluding it. No heat,
so **no tumescent anesthesia**, no thermal nerve-injury risk, and (in many
protocols) no mandatory post-procedure compression stockings. It's the non-thermal
counterpart to [[thermal-venous-ablation]] within [[endovenous-ablation]].

## Why it matters
It targets exactly the burden of thermal methods — eliminating tumescent needle
sticks and the heat-sink requirement. The cost side is a distinctive
**hypersensitivity / phlebitis-type reaction** (a foreign-body response to the
glue) and newer, shorter-term durability data than thermal.

## Strength & caveats
High short/mid-term closure rates; longer-term and head-to-head durability vs.
thermal still maturing. The glue hypersensitivity signal is the characteristic
adverse event to track. Verify current comparative data and cost.

## Connections
- [[endovenous-ablation]] — *subtype-of*: the non-thermal branch.
- [[thermal-venous-ablation]] — *contrasts-with*: trades tumescent/heat burden for a
  glue-reaction risk.
- [[venous-reflux]] — *treats*: closes the refluxing source without heat.

## Questions
- NTNT removes tumescent discomfort but adds a hypersensitivity risk. Construct the
  patient profile for whom this trade clearly favors glue, and the one where it doesn't.
- Durability data is shorter than thermal's. What follow-up duration and endpoint
  would you require before claiming non-inferiority to RFA/laser?
