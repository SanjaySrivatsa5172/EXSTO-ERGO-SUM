# Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) & Post-Thrombotic Syndrome (PTS)

## In my words
**DVT** is thrombus in the deep venous system. **PTS** is its chronic sequela:
months-to-years later the limb develops pain, swelling, heaviness, skin changes
and sometimes ulceration. The mechanism is venous hypertension produced by *two*
post-thrombotic problems at once — residual venous **obstruction** and
**secondary valvular reflux** (valves damaged by the clot/recanalization). PTS
severity is commonly graded with the **Villalta score**.

## Why it matters
PTS is the bridge showing [[chronic-venous-insufficiency]] can be *secondary*
(post-thrombotic), combining the obstruction axis ([[may-thurner-syndrome]]-like)
with the reflux axis ([[venous-reflux]]). That dual mechanism is exactly why
treating only superficial reflux under-serves a post-thrombotic limb.

## Strength & caveats
DVT well defined; PTS is clinically diagnosed (Villalta) with imperfect inter-rater
reliability and overlap with primary CVI. Predicting *who* develops PTS after DVT
remains an open problem — verify current risk factors and prevention evidence.

## Connections
- [[chronic-venous-insufficiency]] — *secondary-cause-of*: PTS is post-thrombotic CVI.
- [[venous-reflux]] — *produces (secondary)*: clot damages valves → reflux.
- [[may-thurner-syndrome]] — *predisposes-to*: iliac compression provokes left
  iliofemoral DVT, which then drives PTS.

## Questions
- PTS combines obstruction and reflux. Design a study that apportions a limb's PTS
  severity between the two mechanisms, and name the measurement for each.
- Villalta is subjective and overlaps with primary CVI. How would you keep a PTS
  trial endpoint from being contaminated by pre-existing primary venous disease?
