# Endovenous Ablation

## In my words
Catheter-based closure of an incompetent superficial trunk (commonly the great
saphenous vein) from inside, instead of surgically stripping it. Thermal methods
(radiofrequency, endovenous laser) heat the wall to occlude it; non-thermal
non-tumescent methods (cyanoacrylate glue, mechanochemical) close it without heat
and so without tumescent anaesthesia. The point is to eliminate the source of
[[venous-reflux]] with minimal trauma.

## Why it matters
It shifted varicose-vein treatment to outpatient, walk-in/walk-out care and is the
practical lever for lowering the refluxing column's contribution to
[[chronic-venous-insufficiency]]. Method choice (thermal vs NTNT) trades tumescent
discomfort against cost and specific risks.

## Strength & caveats
Strong evidence base; thermal ablation has long-term occlusion data, NTNT methods
are newer with shorter follow-up. Caveats: closure rate ≠ symptom outcome; ablating
superficial reflux does not address untreated deep obstruction
([[may-thurner-syndrome]]); glue carries a hypersensitivity/phlebitis signal worth
tracking. Verify current comparative-effectiveness numbers before quoting.

## Connections
- [[venous-reflux]] — *treats*: ablation closes the incompetent refluxing trunk.
- [[chronic-venous-insufficiency]] — *intended-effect-on*: lowering the refluxing
  column's pressure contribution.
- [[may-thurner-syndrome]] — *contradicts/limits*: ablating superficial reflux does
  nothing for an obstructive lesion — a source of "treatment failure" that's really
  misdiagnosis.
- [[ambulatory-venous-pressure]] — *should-improve*: AVP/refilling time is the
  hemodynamic test of whether ablation actually worked, beyond closure rate.
