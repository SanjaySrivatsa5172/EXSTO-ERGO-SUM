# Endovenous Laser Ablation (EVLA / EVLT)

## In my words
A **thermal** modality ([[thermal-venous-ablation]]) using laser light delivered by
an intraluminal fiber; absorbed energy heats blood and the vein wall to occlude it.
**Wavelength matters**: older hemoglobin-targeting lasers (810/980 nm) caused more
pain/bruising; higher water-specific wavelengths (1320/1470 nm) and radial-tip
fibers deliver energy more to the wall, reducing perforations and pain. Tumescent
anesthesia required.

## Why it matters
EVLA shows **very high closure rates**, often cited as marginally higher than RFA,
at the cost of (wavelength-dependent) more bruising. The clinically meaningful
comparison is therefore *which laser*, not "laser vs. RFA" in the abstract — a
common confound in the literature.

## Strength & caveats
Robust occlusion evidence. Caveats: outcomes are strongly wavelength/fiber/energy
(LEED — linear endovenous energy density) dependent, so pooling heterogeneous laser
studies is misleading. Thermal-specific risks as for all thermal methods. Verify
LEED and wavelength specifics.
