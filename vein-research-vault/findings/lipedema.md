# Lipedema

## In my words
A disorder of *symmetric, bilateral* subcutaneous fat deposition, almost always in
women, affecting legs (and often arms) but characteristically **sparing the feet**
(a "cuff" at the ankle). It is painful, tender, bruises easily, and the swelling is
**non-pitting** and does not respond to elevation — distinguishing it from venous
oedema. It is neither primarily venous nor lymphatic, though late disease can
develop secondary lymphatic overload (lipo-lymphedema).

## Why it matters
It's a major *confounder* in venous severity work: a lipedema limb looks "swollen"
and can inflate clinical/QoL scores without any change in venous hemodynamics.
Misattributing lipedema swelling to venous reflux pollutes both diagnosis and trial
outcomes — foot-sparing and non-pitting are the cheap discriminators.

## Strength & caveats
Clinical diagnosis, no validated biomarker; frequently misdiagnosed as obesity or
[[lymphedema]]. Diagnostic criteria are evolving — verify against current consensus.
