# Lymphedema

## In my words
Swelling from failure of *lymphatic* transport, so protein-rich fluid accumulates
in the interstitium. Early it pits; over time it becomes fibrotic and non-pitting
with skin thickening. A positive **Stemmer sign** (can't pinch a skinfold at the
base of the second toe) is a useful bedside marker, and unlike venous oedema and
lipedema it typically **involves the foot/toes**. Primary (developmental) vs.
secondary (post-surgical, infection, filariasis, or chronic venous overload).

## Why it matters
Chronic venous hypertension can overwhelm lymphatic drainage, producing combined
**phlebolymphedema** — so lymphatic and venous disease are not cleanly separable in
advanced limbs ([[chronic-venous-insufficiency]]). For measurement, lymphedema is
where **[[bioimpedance-venous-disease|bioimpedance]]** is best validated, which is
why it's relevant to objective fluid quantification across all these conditions.

## Strength & caveats
Staging (ISL) is clinical/ordinal; Stemmer is specific but not perfectly sensitive;
distinguishing pure lymphedema from phlebolymphedema and [[lipedema]] is often
imperfect. Verify imaging (lymphoscintigraphy/ICG) roles.

## Connections
- [[lipedema]] — *differential*: foot-sparing + non-tender favors lipedema; Stemmer +
  foot involvement favors lymphedema.
- [[chronic-venous-insufficiency]] — *overlaps-as*: phlebolymphedema in advanced CVI.
- [[bioimpedance-venous-disease]] — *measured-by*: BIA is most validated here, a model
  for objective limb-fluid measurement.
