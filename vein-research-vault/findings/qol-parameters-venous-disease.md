# Quality of Life: Subjective vs. Objective Parameters of Venous Disease

## In my words
Venous disease is measured on two fundamentally different kinds of axis, and they
disagree more than people expect:

- **Objective** (clinician/instrument): duplex reflux extent and [[reflux-time]],
  global hemodynamics ([[ambulatory-venous-pressure]], air-plethysmography VFI),
  limb-fluid measures ([[bioimpedance-venous-disease]], perometry/water
  displacement), and physician-rated [[vcss]]/[[revised-vcss]].
- **Subjective** (patient-reported, PRO): generic QoL plus venous-specific
  instruments — **VEINES-QOL/Sym, CIVIQ-20, AVVQ** (Aberdeen), and symptom-focused
  **[[vvsymq]]**.

The core fact: these correlate only weakly. Anatomic/hemodynamic severity is a poor
predictor of how a patient feels, and vice versa.

## Why it matters
This is the central methodological problem of venous outcomes research. The endpoint
you pick encodes a value judgment — "fixed the hemodynamics" vs. "patient feels
better" — and they can diverge after the same treatment. A trial that picks the wrong
axis (or buries the discordance) can look positive or null for reasons unrelated to
benefit. The subjective–objective gap is itself a finding worth studying, not error
to be averaged away. This generalizes [[reflux-severity-evaluation]] from reflux to
all of venous disease.

## Strength & caveats
The disease-specific PROs are validated and responsive; generic tools (SF-36) are less
sensitive to venous change. PROs carry recall/affect bias; objective measures carry
operator/technique variance. No single instrument captures both axes — pairing is
required. Verify each instrument's domains and MCID.

## Connections
- [[reflux-severity-evaluation]] — *generalizes*: the subjective/objective split across
  all venous severity, not just reflux.
- [[vcss]] / [[vvsymq]] — *the two axes embodied*: physician-rated vs. patient-reported.
- [[bioimpedance-venous-disease]] — *objective-candidate*: a fast objective swelling
  measure to pair with PROs.
- [[lipedema]] — *confounds*: non-venous swelling distorts both axes.

## Questions
- Treatment improves VFI but not VEINES-QOL. Give three distinct interpretations and the
  data that would distinguish them — and say which you'd pre-register as primary.
- If subjective and objective axes are weakly correlated, is a composite endpoint a
  solution or a way to hide the disagreement? Argue both sides, then decide.
- Design a study whose explicit aim is to *characterize* the subjective–objective gap
  (not treat it as noise). What would a clinically useful result look like?
