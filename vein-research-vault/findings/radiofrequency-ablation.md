# Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA)

## In my words
A **thermal** endovenous modality (a type of [[thermal-venous-ablation]]) that uses
radiofrequency energy to heat the vein wall in controlled segments to a regulated
temperature (~120 °C), denaturing collagen so the vein contracts and occludes.
Catheter pullback is segmental and temperature-feedback controlled (e.g.
ClosureFast). Requires tumescent anesthesia like all thermal methods.

## Why it matters
RFA's controlled, lower peak temperature is the basis of its classic claim vs. early
laser: **less periprocedural pain and bruising** for comparable closure. Whether
that advantage persists against modern higher-wavelength
[[endovenous-laser-ablation]] is an open comparative question.

## Strength & caveats
Strong durability evidence. Caveats: still tumescent-dependent with thermal-specific
risks (EHIT, saphenous/sural nerve paresthesia); the pain advantage vs. modern laser
is narrower than vs. older 810/980 nm lasers — verify head-to-head data.

## Questions
- The RFA-vs-laser pain difference depends heavily on the laser wavelength compared.
  Design a fair comparative trial and state what you'd hold constant.
- Closure rates are similar across thermal methods. What non-efficacy endpoints should
  decide between them, and how would you weight them?
