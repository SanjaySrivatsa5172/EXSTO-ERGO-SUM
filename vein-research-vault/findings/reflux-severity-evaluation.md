# Evaluation of Severity of Venous Reflux Disease

## In my words
There is no single "severity" of reflux — there are four largely independent axes,
and the central problem is that they correlate imperfectly:

1. **Anatomic / duplex extent** — *where and how much* reflux. Reflux duration
   (>0.5s superficial, >1s deep — a threshold, not a gradient), peak reflux
   velocity, refluxing vein diameter, number of incompetent segments, and whether
   reflux is *axial* (continuous junction-to-calf) vs. *segmental*. More segments
   and axial reflux = anatomically worse.
2. **Global hemodynamic burden** — air plethysmography's **venous filling index
   (VFI, mL/s)** quantifies how fast the leg refills via reflux; it grades the
   *whole-limb* severity that a single duplex segment can't, and tracks with
   [[ceap-classification]] class and ulcer risk. Venous refilling time and invasive
   [[ambulatory-venous-pressure]] sit on this axis too.
3. **Clinical severity** — [[ceap-classification]] C-class (descriptive, ordinal)
   plus the **Venous Clinical Severity Score (VCSS)**, which is graded and
   *responsive to change*, so it's the better instrument for tracking treatment.
4. **Patient-reported QoL** — VEINES-QOL/Sym, CIVIQ-20, AVVQ — what the patient
   actually experiences, which can diverge from all of the above.

## Why it matters
Endpoint selection lives or dies on this. A limb can have severe anatomic reflux
(many segments) but modest VFI and few symptoms — or trivial-looking reflux with a
high VFI and an ulcer. Choosing duplex extent when the question is hemodynamic
burden (or vice versa) is how a real treatment effect gets diluted or a null gets
manufactured. The right severity axis depends on the question being asked.

## Strength & caveats
- Duplex reflux *duration* is near-binary, a poor severity *gradient* on its own.
- VCSS is responsive but has subjective elements and inter-rater variability.
- VFI/air plethysmography is technique-sensitive; reference ranges vary.
- AVP is the closest to a hemodynamic gold standard but is invasive, so most work
  uses surrogates that correlate imperfectly.
- **There is no single accepted gold standard for "reflux severity"** — that gap
  is itself the research opportunity.

## Questions
- A trial wants to show ablation "reduces reflux severity." Pick the single primary
  endpoint you'd defend to a skeptical reviewer, and justify it over the other three
  axes given the question of *patient benefit*.
- Anatomic reflux extent and VFI disagree in a given limb. Give two distinct
  physiological reasons that could happen, and the measurement that distinguishes them.
- Design an analysis to test whether VFI predicts ulcer development better than number
  of refluxing segments — what data, what model, what would falsify the hypothesis?
