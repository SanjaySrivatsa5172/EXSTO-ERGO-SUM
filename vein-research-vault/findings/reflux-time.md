# Reflux Time (Venous Reflux Duration)

## In my words
The duplex-ultrasound measurement that *defines* pathological reflux: the duration
of retrograde (reversed) flow after a provocation maneuver (distal cuff
deflation/release or manual augmentation), with the patient standing or reverse-
Trendelenburg. Working thresholds: **>0.5 s** for superficial veins and perforators,
**>1.0 s** for deep femoropopliteal veins (verify locally). Above the cutoff = reflux
present.

## Why it matters
It's the operational definition of [[venous-reflux]] — but it is essentially a
**threshold/binary criterion, not a severity gradient**. That distinction is the
crux of [[reflux-severity-evaluation]]: reflux time tells you *whether* a segment
refluxes, not *how badly* the limb is loaded; peak reflux velocity, vein diameter,
and global indices (VFI/AVP) carry the severity information reflux time alone doesn't.

## Strength & caveats
Standardized and reproducible *if* provocation, posture, and the measured segment are
controlled — all three change the number, so cross-study comparison is fragile. The
0.5/1.0 s cutoffs are conventions chosen for diagnostic separation, not biological
thresholds. Verify method before pooling.

## Connections
- [[venous-reflux]] — *defines*: the diagnostic criterion for the mechanism.
- [[reflux-severity-evaluation]] — *one-axis-input*: the anatomic/diagnostic measure,
  weak as a standalone severity grade.
- [[ambulatory-venous-pressure]] — *contrasts-with*: AVP measures hemodynamic burden,
  reflux time measures presence.

## Questions
- Reflux time is near-binary. Propose a continuous duplex-derived severity metric and
  the outcome you'd validate it against.
- Provocation method changes reflux time. Design a standardization protocol so two sites
  produce comparable values, and identify the largest residual source of variance.
