# Revised VCSS (rVCSS)

## In my words
The 2010 update of the [[vcss]]. It keeps the ten-attribute, 0–3 structure but
**refines the descriptors** to improve reproducibility — clarifying ambiguous
anchors (e.g. pain wording, the definition/presence of varicose veins, edema and
compression-use descriptors) so different raters score the same limb more
consistently. The aim was reliability, not a new construct.

## Why it matters
For research it's the version to use: tighter descriptors mean less measurement
noise, which directly improves a trial's power to detect a real treatment effect on
the clinical-severity axis ([[reflux-severity-evaluation]]).

## Strength & caveats
Improved but not perfect inter-rater reliability; the inherently subjective items
remain physician-judged. Mixing original-VCSS and rVCSS scores across studies (or
within a longitudinal dataset) is a hidden source of bias — confirm which version a
data source used.

## Connections
- [[vcss]] — *revises*: same structure, refined descriptors.
- [[reflux-severity-evaluation]] — *clinical-axis-instrument* (preferred version).
- [[endovenous-ablation]] — *endpoint-for*: common responsive outcome.

## Questions
- Refined descriptors reduce rater noise. Quantitatively, how does cutting measurement
  variance change the sample size you need — and is switching from VCSS to rVCSS worth
  it mid-study?
- If a legacy dataset used original VCSS, what would you do to keep it comparable with
  new rVCSS data?
