# Stasis Dermatitis (Venous Eczema)

## In my words
The inflammatory skin change of chronic venous hypertension: erythematous, scaly,
itchy, sometimes weeping skin of the lower leg/gaiter area, often with
hyperpigmentation from hemosiderin. Mechanistically it's the cutaneous consequence
of sustained venous pressure — capillary leak, fibrin cuffing, iron deposition, and
inflammation. In [[ceap-classification]] it maps to **C4a**, and it sits on the
pathway toward induration (lipodermatosclerosis, C4b) and ulceration (C5–C6).

## Why it matters
It's an early, *visible* marker that a limb's venous hypertension is already
damaging tissue — a clinical signal of [[chronic-venous-insufficiency]] severity and
a precursor to ulcer. It's also a classic mimic: frequently misdiagnosed and treated
as **cellulitis** (stasis dermatitis is usually bilateral, afebrile, itchy not
tender), leading to unnecessary antibiotics.

## Strength & caveats
Clinical diagnosis; the cellulitis misdiagnosis is well documented. Its position on
the C4→C6 trajectory is associative, not a guaranteed progression — verify which
features predict ulceration.

  quantify that risk and the modifiers.
