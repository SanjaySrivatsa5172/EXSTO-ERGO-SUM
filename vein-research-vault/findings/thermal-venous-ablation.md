# Thermal Venous Ablation

## In my words
The heat-based family of endovenous closure: a catheter/fiber delivers thermal
energy to the vein wall, denaturing it so the vein occludes and fibroses. Because
the energy is hot enough to injure surrounding tissue, thermal methods require
**tumescent anesthesia** (a perivenous fluid cuff that anesthetizes and acts as a
heat sink). The two modalities are **[[radiofrequency-ablation]]** and
**[[endovenous-laser-ablation]]**. It is the thermal branch of
[[endovenous-ablation]]; the non-thermal branch is
[[cyanoacrylate-venous-ablation]].

## Why it matters
The defining trade-off is tumescent anesthesia: it's what makes thermal effective
and durable but also what drives periprocedural discomfort, multiple needle sticks,
and bruising. That trade is the whole reason non-thermal methods exist — so any
thermal-vs-NTNT comparison is really a comparison of this trade-off.

## Strength & caveats
Largest long-term occlusion evidence base of the ablation methods. Caveats:
operator/technique dependent, thermal nerve injury and endothermal heat-induced
thrombosis (EHIT) are the characteristic risks — verify rates.

## Connections

## Questions
- Tumescent anesthesia is both the strength and the burden of thermal ablation.
  Design a trial endpoint that isolates the *patient-experience* cost of tumescence
  from the *efficacy* of closure.
- EHIT and nerve injury are thermal-specific risks. How would you power a study to
  detect a difference in these between RFA and laser?
