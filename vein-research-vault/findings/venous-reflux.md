# Venous Reflux (Valvular Incompetence)

## In my words
Normal vein valves are one-way: blood goes up, toward the heart. Reflux is
retrograde flow through incompetent valves — blood falls back down on standing,
loading the distal segment and raising pressure. Duplex ultrasound defines it by
reversed-flow duration after a provocation (commonly >0.5s in superficial veins,
>1s in deep/femoropopliteal as a working threshold — verify locally). It is the
most common mechanism behind [[chronic-venous-insufficiency]].

## Why it matters
Reflux is the *measurable, mappable* target. Where the reflux starts (saphenous
junction, perforators, deep system) dictates the intervention — e.g. it's the
rationale for [[endovenous-ablation]] of an incompetent saphenous trunk. Map the
reflux, treat the source, and the column pressure falls.

## Strength & caveats
The mechanism is solid; the *thresholds* are conventions, not biology — they vary
by vein segment, posture, and provocation method. Reflux can coexist with
obstruction, and treating one without recognizing the other under-treats the limb.

## Connections
- [[chronic-venous-insufficiency]] — *mechanism-for*: reflux drives the ambulatory
  venous hypertension that produces CVI.
- [[endovenous-ablation]] — *treated-by*: ablation eliminates the refluxing source.
- [[ambulatory-venous-pressure]] — *measured-by*: rapid venous refilling time is the
  hemodynamic fingerprint of reflux.
- [[may-thurner-syndrome]] — *confounds*: obstruction can coexist with reflux;
  treating reflux alone under-treats a mixed limb.
- [[reflux-severity-evaluation]] — *graded-by*: how the severity of this mechanism is
  quantified across anatomic, hemodynamic, clinical, and QoL axes.
