# VVSymQ (Varicose Vein Symptom Questionnaire)

## In my words
A **patient-reported outcome (PRO)** for varicose-vein symptoms: a short,
patient-completed instrument capturing the core symptom burden — typically heaviness,
achiness, swelling, throbbing, and itching — as the patient experiences it day to
day. It was developed to regulatory (PRO-qualification) standards, so it measures the
*subjective* axis that physician scores like [[vcss]] miss.

## Why it matters
It's the counterweight to clinician-rated severity. A treatment can close a vein and
improve VCSS yet leave symptoms (or vice versa); VVSymQ measures the thing the patient
actually cares about, and the *gap* between it and objective measures is a substantive
research finding, not noise — see [[qol-parameters-venous-disease]].

## Strength & caveats
Symptom-focused and responsive, but symptoms are weakly correlated with anatomic
reflux severity, so it answers "does the patient feel better," not "is the hemodynamics
fixed." Recall/affect biases apply as with any PRO. Verify the exact item set and
scoring.

## Questions
- VVSymQ and rVCSS can move in opposite directions after treatment. Build the
  decision rule you'd pre-register for which one is the primary endpoint, and defend it.
- Symptoms correlate poorly with reflux extent. What does that imply about using
  symptom relief to justify treating anatomically mild disease?
